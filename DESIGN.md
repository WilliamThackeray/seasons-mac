# Interaction direction

The desktop stays quiet; snow is a material the user can handle. Stable profiles retain their existing shading. Displaced volume enters a bounded, cellular granular field instead of becoming independently flying ellipses. Grains resist compression, flow downward/diagonally through free space, pile against neighbours and window boundaries, and settle back into smooth profiles. The renderer reconstructs the connected material boundary from local occupancy. There is no velocity impulse or scaled airborne blob.

The glove is a gentle 24-point tool. The shovel is a rigid 36-point exclusion area and clears all contacted static/loose snow from its radius, including when held still, with source-owned fallback if geometry or the resource budget leaves nowhere to place material. Both push area instead of destroying it. The selected tool is visible in the snowflake menu and persisted.

Newly appearing windows erase covered material. An existing window moving from below or either side pushes material ahead of its swept leading boundary. Outward resizing follows the appropriate entering face; retreating faces release rather than pull. A window's own cap remains attached. Support transforms and body motion are committed atomically, and Space changes explicitly clear the scene.

An attached cap is carried material rather than exempt geometry. Its moving columns sweep against other persistent bodies on the same presentation plane before attachment transforms. Contacts split material into bounded vertical samples; only intercepted area detaches to the approach side. Relative continuous contacts account for other moving/resizing bodies and rounded boundaries. A side or underside met before a lower support takes precedence over the downward handoff. Newly detached contacts enter the existing granular field after boundary motion, so the carrier cannot push its stopped snow twice. Exhaustion retains area independently of its former support, preventing hidden snow from resurfacing when the carrier moves back.

A descending cap that meets another window changes support at that contact. Its area is captured before attachment movement, then deposited on the first crossed receiving top after body displacement. Only overlapping columns change owner. Exactly aligned tops remain exposed; receiver saturation uses granular spill and an explicit bounded retry reserve rather than hiding or deleting the cap.

Screen-edge tiling suggestions from WindowManager are excluded before physical scene construction. They do not clear, displace, shadow, or collect snow. A real application resized into the same half-screen rectangle remains a normal moving boundary.

Spotlight is a temporary elevated support. Its rounded attached cap stays visible over ordinary apps through a small cap-only strip that shares the existing Metal frame, mesh, and command buffer. The main frame manually enters the paused strip's draw cycle so MetalKit advances and releases its drawable each frame. The strip has no independent simulation, timer, or falling-flake pass; it is no wider than the display and no taller than 128 points. Exact public owner-name fallback covers missing Spotlight process-role cache entries without reading search contents or window titles. Lower-plane app geometry cannot erase or push the attached cap. If underlying native windows block granular placement while brushing, snow can redistribute along the attached support. Dismissal releases exposed columns into the normal desktop material field; portions covered by native windows on that plane are removed. Remaining exposed area falls normally and uses a bounded reserve if granular storage is busy. New-window coverage clips retained column intervals. Repeated snapshots cannot duplicate released area, and explicit scene clears discard any reserve.

Windward corners of protruding walls receive snowfall faster and can use more of the existing height allowance. Direct snowfall is suppressed in the lee corner over a distance proportional to horizontal wind speed (up to 96 points). Reversing wind swaps the incoming-snowfall pattern without moving, clamping, or deleting existing piles. Physical capacity is independent of wind: swept snow, window-boundary pushes, support handoffs, and loose grains can enter and settle throughout the snowfall shadow. Initial dusting and decorative flake landings use the incoming-snowfall mask. Effects are cached per support when wind or geometry changes. Coplanar tops are not treated as protruding walls.

The wind control follows the user's arrow sketch: a rounded, neutral native material, quiet dot grid, and a single accent-colored arrow. Arrow angle controls direction and length controls strength. Dragging follows the pointer directly with a bounded rubber-band beyond full length and thickness feedback while stretching/compressing. A short damped spring returns presentation to the stored strength on release. The native transient popover supplies entry/exit motion and turns that off under Reduce Motion. Outside clicks, Close, and Escape dismiss it. A release-spring timer is invalidated at rest, reset, or dismissal; Reduce Motion snaps the arrow immediately. Keyboard changes are immediate. Reset returns to calm. Outside its brief settling spring, the pad redraws for events only. Native labels, accent colors, popover material, keyboard focus, and VoiceOver actions follow macOS appearance/accessibility settings.

The wind pad sits in the snowflake panel rather than in a permanently floating utility window. Direction/strength are applied to both airborne flakes and detached snow, and saved with existing preferences. The only explanatory line is the direct drag instruction; no implementation details appear in the product.

# Geometry decisions

Snow receives direct ambient deposition only in sky-exposed spans on its presentation plane. Visible support spans are separate, so swept snow may physically reach a floor under a floating window without generating new snowfall through its roof. Profiles follow rounded upper contours, and continuous body collisions use flat faces plus corner arcs. Spotlight's search panel uses a 28-point radius estimate and ignores lower-plane app/widget/Dock masks for its attached cap. Widget host margins are trimmed before building either the support or collider. Metadata has no exact visible-content frame or radius; widget insets and per-kind radii are explicit estimates informed by the supplied crops. Vertical Docks are excluded from supports, bodies, and snowfall shadows. Real horizontal bottom Dock frames take precedence, including nearly full-width trays. Configured orientation, Dock-level geometry, and a retained valid native tray ID prevent auxiliary animation windows from displacing the tray. If compact geometry is missing, the live bottom reservation and bounded item-count width supply a fallback.

Missing or transparent Dock records no longer leave the bottom Dock without collisions. The app refreshes each screen's visible bottom reservation at existing metadata cadence. A real reservation of at least 20 points can supply a bounded bottom roof without a Quartz tray record; the auto-hide reveal strip cannot. Compact metadata refines horizontal extent, while tile counts supply a bounded width estimate when no footprint exists. Opaque tray geometry still takes precedence, and explicit side orientation excludes every fallback. A canonical support/body identity preserves cap continuity while the native window ID remains separate for candidate selection.

# Verification limits

Bottom Dock hover shrink releases profile area that can no longer fit on its shorter width or tapered shoulders. It uses the existing granular field and bounded reserve, preserving area without adding a separate Dock solver. Repeated snapshots do not release the same area twice. Vertical Dock records are rejected even when a missing process-role entry would otherwise classify their negative-level windows as widgets.

The supplied screenshots and sketch were inspected directly. Source and headless regressions cover granular conservation/flow/compression, connected contour bounds, glove/shovel exclusion, new-versus-moving window behavior, saturation, rounded collisions, attachment, resource bounds, and wind/stretch math. Native UI and gesture feel have not been visually exercised, because the user requested no app launch/computer-control tools or benchmarks. No independent visual score is claimed. Native system controls/materials minimize custom assumptions, but exact host-frame alignment still needs the user's target desktop to verify.


## Installer and welcome

The installer uses the browser desktop’s midnight blue, Aqua arc, and winter ridges. Finder supplies the two real drag targets; the background supplies the instruction and arrow. The same glossy snowflake marks the app and mounted disk. Artwork is drawn offscreen at build time, and the image carries its own Finder layout and Retina background.

The welcome window uses a standard macOS title bar and native buttons. A framed preview on the left illustrates each step, with short instructions on the right. The desktop and wind previews animate once per visit and respect Reduce Motion. A version-independent preference records the presentation, so closing or skipping is final. Existing saved weather settings also suppress the welcome.
